02 — How AI fits into my process

One tool, one job, at each stage

Each tool has a specific role at a specific stage of the design process. The decisions stay with me.

01ResearchPerplexity

Fast, cited background on a new domain — how an industry works, common patterns, and what competitors do.

What stays with me: talking to real users. Perplexity tells me what's known; people tell me what's actually happening.

02SynthesisClaude · NotebookLM

NotebookLM answers only from my own research notes, with citations, so insights stay grounded in what users actually said. Claude helps me cluster themes and spot contradictions across sessions.

What stays with me: deciding which insight matters most.

03IdeationFigma

Exploring directions visually, using Figma's AI features for quick first drafts and variants.

What stays with me: choosing the direction and weighing the trade-offs.

04PrototypingClaude

Turning sketches and ideas into interactive, working prototypes I can click through and refine, one screen at a time. The animations on this site started this way.

What stays with me: the interaction details, and whether it feels right.

05FeedbackClaude

Critiquing my own designs before testing ("what could go wrong here?"), and summarising feedback from reviews and user tests into clear patterns.

What stays with me: real feedback comes from real users. Claude helps me prepare for it and make sense of it, but it doesn't replace it.

05 — What stays human

The parts I don't hand over

User empathy
AI can summarise what users said. It can't weigh what they meant against what they need — that takes watching real people struggle with an interface.
Final design judgment
AI generates options. It can't tell which one is right for this product, this user, and this moment in the roadmap. That call is mine.
Checking the facts
AI can sound confident and still be wrong. I only publish numbers and claims I can explain and verify myself.
